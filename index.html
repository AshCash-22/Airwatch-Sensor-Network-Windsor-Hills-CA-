Why this exists

The reasoning behind the project, and behind what's on this page.

The gap

Los Angeles County spans over 4,000 square miles, but its official air monitoring network is sparse. Many neighborhoods — including this one — sit miles from the nearest official station, which means residents have no reliable way to know what they're actually breathing on a given day. AirWatch is a small, low-cost station built to close that gap for one specific place, rather than waiting on county-wide coverage that may never arrive at this scale.

What it isn't

This isn't lab-grade regulatory equipment, and it doesn't claim to be. It's a low-cost sensor built to catch trends and give residents a real, local reference point — a supplement to official monitoring, not a replacement for it.

On the hardware An ESP32 microcontroller reads a laser particulate sensor (PMS5003) and an environmental gas sensor (BME680), then pushes readings over WiFi every 30–60 seconds.
On the data Every reading is timestamped and kept, so trends over hours, days, and weeks are what this station is actually for — not just a single live number.
On this page Built to be readable by a neighbor, not just an engineer — plain labels, plain explanations, updated automatically.
